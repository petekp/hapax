Elaborate ornamentation in vocal music: runs, trills, leaps, and rapid passagework that decorate a melodic line, or the agile voice able to sing them. **Colorature** is the older, French-shaped spelling of coloratura, and it names the same thing: the moment a melody stops walking and starts to glitter.

> *The aria's final colorature climbed clear of the orchestra, every note distinct as cut glass.*

## Etymology

From French **colorature**, from Italian **coloratura** "coloring," from Late Latin **coloratura**, ultimately from Latin **colorare** "to color." The ornament was understood as color laid over a plainer line. The spelling *colorature* survives mainly in older English writing on song.

## Related Words

- **coloratura** (the standard modern spelling; also the soprano who sings it)
- **fioritura** (a florid embellishment of a melody; literally "flowering")
- **melisma** (several notes sung to a single syllable)
- **roulade** (a quick run of notes sung on one syllable)
